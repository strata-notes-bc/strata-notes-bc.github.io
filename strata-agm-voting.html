Strata AGMs and voting: majority, 3/4 and unanimous resolutions explained

What each type of strata vote is used for, quorum and proxies, how votes are counted, and why showing up to the AGM matters.

Most big decisions in a strata happen at general meetings. The annual general meeting (AGM) approves the budget and elects council; special general meetings handle levies, bylaw changes and major projects. Knowing which vote applies tells you how hard a decision is to pass, and how much your vote counts.

The types of vote

ResolutionThresholdUsed for
MajorityMore than half of votes castBudget, election of council, EV charging infrastructure, many routine matters
3/4 voteAt least 75% of votes castSpecial levies, bylaw amendments, most spending from the CRF, significant changes to common property
80% vote80% of all eligible votesWinding up the strata for sale
UnanimousEvery eligible voteChanging unit entitlement, some other fundamental changes

Most thresholds count only votes cast by owners present in person or by proxy, not all owners. That makes attendance decisive.

With 40 of 60 owners present, a special levy needs 30 votes, not 45. A motivated group can pass or block a levy if others stay home.

Quorum

Unless bylaws say otherwise, quorum is one-third of eligible voters, present in person or by proxy. If quorum is not reached within 30 minutes, the meeting is often adjourned for a week, and those present then constitute quorum.

Proxies

Owners can appoint anyone as proxy (with limits on employees of the strata manager). Proxies can be general or come with instructions. Collecting proxies is how levies and bylaw changes are often won or lost, so read any proxy request carefully.

Who votes

Each strata lot generally has one vote, though bylaws can assign votes by unit entitlement for some purposes. Tenants vote only if the landlord assigns voting rights or for certain matters under long-term leases. Owners in arrears on a lien may lose the right to vote on 3/4 resolutions.

Why it matters when buying

The AGM minutes show how contentious decisions are, who attends, and whether levies pass easily. A building where levies routinely fail may have deferred maintenance building up. For strata price trends across Metro Vancouver, see EstateBlock's townhouse price guide.

Questions people ask

Can I vote by email?

Electronic meetings and voting are allowed if bylaws permit or under rules adopted since 2020; check your bylaws.

How much notice must the strata give for an AGM?

At least two weeks' written notice, with the agenda and proposed resolutions.

Can tenants vote?

Only if the landlord assigns voting rights, or for limited matters under long-term leases.

Does an abstention count against a 3/4 vote?

Abstentions are generally not counted as votes cast.

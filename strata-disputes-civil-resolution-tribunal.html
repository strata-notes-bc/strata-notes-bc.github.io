Strata disputes and the Civil Resolution Tribunal: how the process works

Common strata disputes, trying to resolve them first, how the Civil Resolution Tribunal handles strata claims, timelines, costs and what orders it can make.

Disagreements in stratas are common: noise, leaks, bylaw fines, parking, renovations, access to records, and how council spends money. BC's Civil Resolution Tribunal (CRT) is the online tribunal that decides most strata disputes, and it is designed so owners can use it without a lawyer.

Start with the strata

Before filing, put your complaint in writing to council. Many disputes end when council sees the issue documented. You can also request a hearing with council under section 34.1 of the Act; council must hold it within four weeks and give a written decision within one week after.

When to go to the CRT

The CRT handles most strata property disputes, including:

  • Bylaw enforcement and fines
  • Repairs and maintenance responsibility
  • Chargebacks of insurance deductibles
  • Access to records and documents
  • Significantly unfair actions by council or owners
  • Disputes between owners, tenants and the strata

Some matters stay with the courts, such as certain claims about land title, and the CRT can refuse claims better suited to court.

The process

StageWhat happens
ApplicationFile online, pay the fee, describe the dispute and what you want
NoticeOther party is served and has a set time to respond
FacilitationA case manager helps both sides try to settle
DecisionIf not settled, a tribunal member decides on written evidence
EnforcementOrders can be filed in court and enforced like court orders

Most strata disputes take a few months. Fees are modest and reduced if you file online; the CRT publishes current fees.

Building a strong claim

  • Keep emails, letters, photos and dates.
  • Quote the specific bylaw or section of the Act.
  • Show you raised it with council first.
  • Ask for a specific, practical remedy: repair the leak, reverse the fine, provide records.

Reading past decisions

CRT decisions are published. Searching for your issue ("flooring noise", "chargeback deductible", "records request") shows how similar disputes were decided, which is the best guide to whether your claim is worth bringing.

Questions people ask

Do I need a lawyer for the CRT?

No. It is built for people to represent themselves; lawyers are only allowed in limited cases.

Can the CRT cancel a bylaw fine?

Yes, if the strata did not follow proper process or the fine was not justified.

How long does a CRT strata dispute take?

Often a few months, depending on complexity and settlement.

Are CRT decisions binding?

Yes. Final orders can be enforced through the courts.

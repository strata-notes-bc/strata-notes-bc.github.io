EV charging in BC stratas: right to charge, votes and costs

How BC's strata EV rules work, electrical planning reports, majority votes for charging infrastructure, who pays, and what charging costs compared with gas.

More strata owners want to charge an electric vehicle at home, and BC changed the law in 2023 to make that easier. The details matter: who can install what, how votes work, and how electricity is billed.

What changed

Amendments to the Strata Property Act in 2023:

  • Lowered the vote needed to approve EV charging infrastructure and electrical planning reports from three-quarters to a majority vote.
  • Let owners request permission to install a charger at their own cost, with the strata able to refuse only on reasonable grounds set out in regulation (for example, insufficient electrical capacity or if it conflicts with a planned building-wide system).
  • Required larger stratas to obtain an electrical planning report on a phased schedule, to understand capacity for future charging.

Two approaches

Individual chargers. An owner pays to run a circuit to their stall, usually on a separate meter or with a smart charger that tracks use. Works in buildings with spare capacity and few EVs.

Building-wide system. The strata installs a load-managed network that can serve many stalls, often with costs recovered through user fees. Better long term, but requires a bigger upfront decision and often financing or rebates.

Who pays for electricity

Owners using shared power must pay for it. Common methods: networked chargers that bill users, sub-meters, or flat monthly fees approved by bylaw. Stratas cannot let some owners charge on common electricity for free indefinitely.

What it saves

At 16,000 km a year, home charging costs about $346 against roughly $2,240 for gasoline. That gap is why EV access increasingly affects how buyers value a strata parking stall.

Before you buy

Ask whether the building has an electrical planning report, whether chargers are permitted or already installed, and whether your stall is assigned (limited common property) or allocated by rule. A stall that can never be wired is worth less to a growing share of buyers.

Questions people ask

Can my strata refuse my EV charger request?

Only on reasonable grounds set by regulation, such as electrical capacity or conflict with a planned system.

What vote is needed for building-wide charging?

A majority vote for EV charging infrastructure since the 2023 changes.

Who pays for the electricity?

The EV owner, through metering, networked billing or approved fees.

What is an electrical planning report?

A study of the building's electrical capacity and options for EV charging, required for larger stratas on a phased schedule.
